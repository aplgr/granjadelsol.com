The title is the official document identifying the legal owner of the property. It is required to sell or transfer the land. Without a valid title, a legally binding sale cannot take place.

A property purchase in Paraguay generally involves several steps:
  1. First Appointment with the Escribana (Notary Public):
    • Sign a preliminary purchase agreement.
    • Make a down payment.
    • The escribana formally verifies the property title (approximately 2–4 weeks).

  2. Second Appointment with the Escribana:
    • Sign the final purchase agreement.
    • Pay the remaining purchase price.
    • The escribana begins the official ownership transfer (approximately 4–8 weeks).

Once full payment has been made, the buyer becomes the new owner and can generally use the property immediately. Issuing the new title is then an administrative formality. When it is ready, the escribana contacts the new owner so the document can be collected.

In addition to the purchase price, the buyer is responsible for closing costs. These mainly include the legal transfer costs and, if a real estate agent becomes involved, the agent's commission.

Property transfer costs

The legal transfer costs are paid through the escribana (notary). They are regulated by law and, depending on the purchase price, are generally between approximately 1.5% and 4.5% of the purchase price. The escribana calculates the exact amount in advance.

These costs generally include:

  • Notarial fees: Preparation and notarization of the purchase agreement and the required steps for transferring ownership.
  • Property registry fees: Registration of the buyer as the new owner.
  • Taxes: Government or municipal taxes may apply depending on the property's location and purchase price.
  • Administrative fees: Charges for processing by cadastral, tax and other public authorities.
